# Target Architecture Reference: gfx950 / CDNA4 / MI350X / MI355X

> Specializes the [CK DSL Optimization Runbook](../optimization_runbook.md).
> Sibling arches: see [arch/README.md](README.md).

The runbook itself is architecture-agnostic — every principle and
lever applies to any AMDGPU CDNA target. This file collects the
**gfx950 / CDNA4** specifics that the DSL targets by default. When
porting to gfx942 (MI300X), gfx940 (MI250X-class CDNA3), or gfx90a,
the corresponding caps and intrinsics differ — always verify against
the vendor spec sheet. (Section numbers below preserve the runbook's
historical `21.x` numbering so existing cross-references resolve.)

> **Backend-validity note.** Perf and register/occupancy numbers are
> backend-sensitive. The **production** backend is **llvm22 + comgr 7.2**
> (selected by importing `torch` first, so the bundled
> `torch/lib/libamd_comgr.so` resolves). Some pre-2026-06-19 measurements
> were taken on an **llvm20 + comgr 7.0.1** backend (when a dependency
> dlopened the system comgr before torch); on llvm20 the d128 attention
> register/occupancy and prefill-gap picture was materially worse. Such
> numbers are corrected inline below to the llvm22 values. Correctness /
> byte-identity / ISA-availability facts are backend-independent and
> unchanged.
>
> **Recommendation: always build + benchmark on `llvm22`** (import torch / a
> ROCm-7.2 toolchain *first* so comgr 7.2 loads; confirm `ROCKE_LLVM_FLAVOR`
> resolves to `llvm22`). The flavor is *not* perf-neutral — MFMA scheduling
> and VGPR/AGPR allocation differ, so `llvm20` can report **false**
> register-bound / AGPR-spill / 1-WG/CU regressions that vanish on `llvm22`.

### 21.1 MFMA Atom Catalog (gfx950)

Native 32x32x64 scaled MFMA is exposed through the target catalog's
`mfma_scaled` family, separately from the `MfmaAtom` factories below. It
supports independent FP8/BF8/FP6/BF6/FP4 inputs and K32 E8M0 scales. See
[MMA entrypoints and dispatcher coverage](../../reference/mma_metadata.md#gfx950-scaled-mfma-paths)
and the [untuned scaled-GEMM examples](../../../python/rocke/examples/gfx950/gemm/README.md).

`IRBuilder.mfma_scale_mx` (`tile.mfma_scale_mx`, Python engine only) exposes
the same instruction family with every immediate free: the 16x16x128 and
32x32x64 shapes, independent `cbsz`/`blgp` formats, operands sized to their
format (`<8|6|4 x i32>`, so an fp4 operand takes four VGPRs), and `op_sel`
(0..3) choosing the byte of each scale dword, so one dword carries the scales of
four K steps. Lane maps are in `core/arch/mfma_scale_mx.py`; the 16x16x128 fp8
operand is two 16-byte halves 64 K apart. Facts verified on gfx950:

- The f8f6f4 MFMA does not keep full fp32 precision inside one instruction's
  dot product: on full-range random fp8 data the result differs from an fp64
  reference by up to about 2^-12 of the sum of |products| (exact-arithmetic
  fixtures are bitwise exact). Compare routes bitwise, not against fp64 within
  fp32 ulps.
- Inline-asm MFMAs are opaque to the hazard recognizer: pad with `s_nop` after
  a VALU writes any of their source registers (without it the result was
  wrong) and before any non-MFMA consumer of the result. A chain of dependent
  asm MFMAs on one accumulator needs no padding between them.
- Inline-asm VMEM is also opaque to the VALU-writes-SGPR hazard: a `v_readlane` (spilled-SGPR restore) or
  `v_readfirstlane` writing an SGPR that the next instructions use as the `saddr` / base of an asm `global_*` or
  `buffer_*` needs 5 wait states, which LLVM inserts only before its own VMEM instructions. Measured on gfx950 (Qwen3.8
  large-batch probe P-L4, `library/kernels/gfx950/qwen38_mono/large_batch/moe_lb.py`): grid-barrier arrivals through
  `device_ops.atomic_add_scoped` landed at a wrong address (barrier timeouts) or faulted
  (`MEMORY_APERTURE_VIOLATION`), depending on register allocation. Start such asm with `s_nop 4` (moe_lb `hz`).
- A compiler-emitted MFMA (XDL) writing the accumulator or an operand right
  before an asm MFMA chain (routes mixed on one accumulator) needs XDL-write
  wait states, far more than the VALU case; `mx_mma_chain` raises the lead to
  24 wait states when it sees an MFMA-defined source, and a loop-carried
  accumulator fed by a compiler MFMA needs `AsmHazard(after_xdl=True)`. The
  asm chain's tail padding covers a compiler MFMA reading its result.
  In the mixed-route probes the compiler copied the accumulator from AGPRs
  to VGPRs ahead of the asm (the asm takes VGPR operands), which already
  supplied the wait states, and the result was correct without the XDL lead;
  the lead is kept as a conservative default, not a proven requirement.
- Inline-asm DPP moves and `v_permlane16_swap_b32` need `s_nop 1` ahead of
  them: a VALU write followed by a DPP or permlane-swap read of that VGPR
  needs two wait states on gfx950, and the compiler cannot see into the asm.
- Scale-register refresh is expensive (measured on MI355X, Qwen3.8 large-batch probe P-L3,
  `library/kernels/gfx950/qwen38_mono/large_batch/gemm.py`): a loop of scaled MFMAs whose scale VGPRs are
  rewritten for every MFMA (a fresh block-scale dword per token tile, whether produced by a VALU shift, an LDS
  read or a global load, at any prefetch depth) ran about 8x slower than the same loop with scale registers
  that are not rewritten inside the loop (same MFMA count and operand traffic). Packing four consecutive K
  steps' scales of a lane's block into one dword and selecting the byte with `op_sel` (one scale write per
  four K steps; `GemmKnobs(sop=True)`) recovered it, bit-identical results. Budget scale writes per MFMA when
  designing MX kernels with many independent accumulators.
- The pre-existing fixed-format `mfma_scale_f32_16x16x128_f8f6f4` op (P15)
  fails to compile at the `llvm22` flavor (its call has the older 11-operand
  form); `mfma_scale_mx` emits the 9-operand overloaded intrinsic, verified
  to compile and run at `llvm22` and `llvm23` (`llvm20` not run). Fixing P15
  changes the emitted IR of a dual-engine op, so it needs the C++ engine
  changed in the same step; it is an open item, not done here. Use
  `mfma_scale_mx` for new code.
- `<6 x i32>` (fp6) values occupy 32 bytes in LLVM's memory layout: a
  per-lane fp6 operand array loaded as `<6 x i32>` elements has a 32-byte stride.

The `compute` knob of `library/kernels/gfx950/mx_mfma.py` switches between
the catalog atom (dual engine), this op, and an inline-asm chain.

The full DSL atom catalog lives in `helpers/atoms.py::MFMA_*_ATOMS`.
On gfx950 the following are available:

Lane-layout matching across chained atoms (§7.2): the 16×16 atoms'
C-output doesn't natively match their A-input, so a QK→PV-style
chain needs an LDS round-trip or a cross-lane permute. The 32×32
atoms natively match — prefer them when the chain pattern allows
(`use_mfma_32x32`, `use_transposed_qk_32x32` on the attention 2D path).

### 21.2 LDS specifics

| Spec | gfx950 | gfx942 (MI300X) | gfx90a (MI250X) |
|---|---|---|---|
| LDS per CU | 160 KB | 64 KB | 64 KB |
| LDS banks | 64 | 32 | 32 |
| Preferred swizzle (§6.4a) | padding | XOR | XOR |
| `ds_read_b64_tr_b16` / `ds_read_b128_tr_b16` | CDNA4 only | — | — |
| `ds_read_tr_b8` (i8 / fp8 / bf8 transposed) | CDNA4 only | — | — |
| `ds_read_b64`, `ds_read_b128` conflict period | 64 dwords (256 bytes) | 32 dwords (128 bytes) | 32 dwords (128 bytes) |
| `ds_read_b32`, `ds_write_b{32,64,128}` conflict period | 32 dwords (128 bytes) | 32 dwords (128 bytes) | 32 dwords (128 bytes) |
| Intra-dword sub-dword accesses | conflict-free | conflict-free | conflict-free |
| Unaligned `ds_read_u16` charged to | `SQ_LDS_IDX_ACTIVE` (not `SQ_LDS_BANK_CONFLICT`) | same | same |

The conflict periods summarize historical wave64 observations for the listed
b32, b64, and b128 operations. On gfx950, b64 and b128 reads use a 64-dword
address-class period; their writes use 32 dwords. Reads and writes may therefore
need different swizzle strategies. A matching address class produces a conflict
only for distinct addresses in the same opcode-specific lane phase. See the
[gfx950 predictor guide](../utilities/skills/lds-bank-conflict-expert/references/gfx950.md)
for the exact phase keys and supported request scope, and its
[validation boundaries](../utilities/skills/lds-bank-conflict-expert/references/validation-boundaries.md)
before interpreting these rules as evidence about hardware behavior or timing.

**Lever: a wide-K consumer of a relabelled accumulator.** When a 16x16x32
MFMA consumes two stacked 16x16 C tiles relabelled into its A operand (the
attention backward's `P^T dO` / `dS^T Q`), the A operand holds its 32 K values
in a permuted order (K = `16 t + 4 G + r` in catalog slot `8 G + 4 t + r`),
and the B operand must be loaded with the same permutation. Because
`ds_read_b64_tr_b16` takes a per-lane address, that permuted B is two ordinary
16x16x16 B transpose reads 16 K rows apart: no extra LDS read against the
16x16x16 consumer, half the MFMA instructions. Prove the read plan against the
operand map on every lane at build time (the backward's `tr16_read_plan`);
the bank-conflict pattern of the second read differs from the catalog K = 32
read, so re-check the pads of the B tile.

#### 21.4a Worked example: the d128 attention-prefill lever is `iglp_opt` + `num_warps=4` (paired) — read the limiter, then move both knobs

The single-batch d128 attention prefill combo (`UnifiedAttention2DTiledSpec`,
`head_size=128`, `num_seqs=1`, no-FP8, no-SW, `max_seqlen_q>256`) is the
canonical case where **reading the limiter correctly chooses the lever**.
The shipped selector routed this cohort to `num_warps=2` with no
interleave, which left the Matrix cores starved: rocprofv3 on D128 S8192
(Llama-3.1-405B, bf16) showed **MFMA-busy ≈5% / occupancy ≈12% at 4
waves/CU**. The limiter is *both* occupancy and MFMA scheduling, and a joint
`num_warps × iglp_opt` sweep found the win path moves both knobs together:
`use_softmax_mfma_interleave` (one `iglp_opt` at the loop top — §21.3,
runbook §8.4) **plus `num_warps=4`**. This is now the production routing
(`_enable_softmax_mfma_interleave`); rocprofv3 confirms the mechanism —
MFMA-busy ≈5%→18%, occupancy ≈12%→24%.

The widening is occupancy-**better**, not a tradeoff — it reaches 8 waves/CU
by *lowering* register pressure:

| Config (d128, production llvm22) | VGPR | AGPR | spill | LDS | waves/CU |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `num_warps=2`, no interleave (prior shipped) | 296 | 40 | 0 | 64 KB | 4 |
| **+ `use_softmax_mfma_interleave` + `num_warps=4` (winning config)** | **250** | **0** | 0 | 64 KB | **8** |

Both numbers were extracted with `llvm-readelf` on the produced HSACO
(throttle-independent — the primary occupancy signal on a clock-throttled
box; see runbook §10.6). Because nw=4 *drops* VGPR (296→250) and clears the
AGPR file (40→0), it does **not** trip the `_enable_d128_small_tile` nw=2
occupancy reconciliation (that concern was `T=32` on llvm20; here `T` stays
128, LDS stays 64 KB, well under the 160 KB/CU budget — the kernel is
VGPR-bound, not LDS-bound). Modes 0/1 (`iglp_opt` at the loop top) are ported
to the gfx950 C twin; mode 2 is Python-only and rejected by the C path.

End-to-end through production auto-dispatch this is **3.6x at S8192** (122→443
TF) and holds 1.9–3.6x across S512–S8192, across the model cohort
(Llama-3/3.1, Qwen2.5/3, GPT-3-13B MHA) and both bf16/fp16, correctness
`max_abs = 0.0` vs the shipped kernel. **Escape hatch:**
`HIPDNN_GFX950_D128_SOFTMAX_INTERLEAVE=0` restores the prior nw=2 routing.
The residual gap to FlyDSL (≈1090 TF) is structural (an 8-wave / 32×32
dual-wave rewrite) and tracked separately; this is the hint-only ceiling.
The cohort-facing routing table and the full eligible-space sweep (why the
lever is gated to *this* cohort and not d64 / multi-seq / sliding-window /
d256) live in the
[attention harness README](../../../../library/builders/gfx950/attention/README.md).

> **Backend caveat.** An earlier llvm20 measurement of a related
> `BLOCK_M=128` `use_q_direct_reg` body reported **256 VGPR with spills and
> only 1 WG/CU**, and motivated an LDS-cut occupancy story (a `T=block_size`
> "small tile" vs a `T=64` K-single-buffer lever, with a 229 / 173 / 215
> VGPR table). That occupancy wall was **an llvm20 backend artifact**. The
> `HIPDNN_GFX950_D128_SMALL_TILE` escape hatch and the
> single-buffer/small-tile levers remain in the tree but are **not** the
> production lever; the shipped path is the `iglp_opt` + `num_warps=4` combo
> above. The `use_agpr_alloc_zero` / mfma-vgpr-form lever is bit-accurate but
> **not** the lever here on llvm22 (it leaves the MFMA-idle gap unchanged).

### 21.8 Compiler caveats specific to gfx950

- **`s_sched_barrier` is a compile-time scheduling fence, not a
  reliably-emitted runtime instruction — do not read "0 in the ISA
  bucket" as "no effect".** Earlier notes said the backend "silently
  removes" `s_sched_barrier` / `s_sched_group_barrier` on gfx950; that
  was a misread of the disassembly. `b.sched_barrier(mask)` lowers to
  `llvm.amdgcn.sched.barrier`, which steers the LLVM post-RA scheduler
  *at compile time* and often leaves no corresponding instruction in the
  object — `probe_isa_inspect.py` reporting a `sched_barrier` sub-bucket
  of 0 confirms the runtime instruction is absent, not that the
  scheduling constraint was dropped. The fence has a measurable effect:
  placing it between the QK MFMA cluster and the post-QK async prefetch
  steers the scheduler to keep the QK MFMAs packed instead of interleaving
  the next-tile `buffer_load_lds`. An early **llvm20** measurement of the
  single-batch d128 prefill combo at `num_warps==1` recorded a same-session
  **+35–36 %** from this fence (and a −2.5–4.6 % regression at
  `num_warps>=2`, where the extra ILP makes the fence over-constrain the
  scheduler). That win is **both backend-specific and superseded**: on the
  production **llvm22** backend the d128 body is already occupancy-clean at
  `num_warps=2` (§21.4a), and the production schedule lever is
  `use_softmax_mfma_interleave` (an `iglp_opt`), which is mutually exclusive
  with `use_sched_barrier`. So the `sched_barrier` lever is **not on any
  live attention cohort** — but the *mechanism* (a compile-time fence with a
  real effect, distinct from a runtime instruction) is the accurate and
  backend-independent description. The separate
  `sched_group_barrier` directives the compv4/compv3 GEMM pipeline emits
  are net-negative on gfx950 and are off by default (runbook §8.4).
  Always confirm a hint's effect by diffing the mainloop ISA, never by
  perf alone.
- **`-mllvm -enable-post-misched=0`** is safe in CK's CMake-generated
  code but has been observed miscompiling MLIR-generated kernels on
  this arch — treat as risky.
- The remaining recommended CK-style flag stack
  (`-mllvm -amdgpu-function-calls=false`,
  `-mllvm -amdgpu-early-inline-all=true`,
  `-mllvm --lsr-drop-solution=1`, `-fno-offload-uniform-block`,
  `-O3`, fast / unsafe math) has been measured safe but rarely
  moves the needle by more than 1 % on gfx950 — large gaps come
  from kernel structure, not flags (§10.2).
- **An inline-asm load whose result nothing consumes (an L2 "touch") must
  keep its destination allocated until the data lands.** The compiler sees
  no consumer and no `vmcnt` to track, so it reuses the destination VGPRs
  while the load is in flight; on gfx950 this corrupted live registers and
  faulted. End each batch of such loads with one `s_waitcnt vmcnt(0)` asm
  that takes all of the batch's destinations as operands (the layer-kernel
  toy chain's idle-CTA prefetch, `library/kernels/gfx950/qwen38_mono/toy_chain.py`).
- **Persistent multi-stage kernels: give each stage fresh SGPR copies of the
  wave / block ids** (`device_ops.fresh_s`, framework `fresh_ids`). Without
  them, a hop-only stage chain of depth >= 3 miscompiled: the SGPR base of the
  asm mailbox poll was assigned VGPRs (an assembler error on llvm22, a
  null-address fault on llvm23). Refreshing either id alone avoids it.
- **Kernarg preload** (`inreg` kernel parameters; gfx950 preloads up to 16
  user SGPRs at dispatch) removes the entry wait on the kernarg `s_load`s;
  in a short persistent kernel that wait dominated the launch ramp (ATT).
  Python engine only (`ir.PYTHON_ONLY_PARAM_ATTRS`). Do not replace a
  vector load of a counter another kernel just wrote by a scalar
  `s_load_dword ... glc`: its `lgkmcnt` wait was far slower than the vector
  load on gfx950.

| `_DATALAYOUT` in `core/lower_llvm.py` | gfx950 datalayout string baked in |
| Other supported targets | `gfx942` (MI300X), `gfx90a` (MI250X) — verify MFMA atom shapes are valid for the target |

To target a different arch from the default, pass
`isa="amdgcn-amd-amdhsa--gfx942"` (or similar) to `compile_kernel`,
and verify the atoms picked by the spec are in that arch's
`_F16_WARP_TILE_SHAPES_*` / `_BF16_WARP_TILE_SHAPES_*` set.
