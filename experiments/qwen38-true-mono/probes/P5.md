# P5: coherence inside one persistent launch (gfx950)

DESIGN.md 8.2, probe P5: a KV row rewritten on one XCD after other XCDs read it, re-read in the same persistent
launch (the MTP draft forwards of a `round` launch rewrite draft-cache rows that the previous forward read), and a
word written in the launch read back through the scalar path (draft positions, `L`, slots: the mono reads its tables
with `s_buffer_load`, `stages/common.py` `table_entry`). Gate: both negative controls fail, the positives pass. This
file gives ratios and counts only. Absolute device times are in
`hackathon-prep/private-perf/qwen38-true-mono/probes/P5.md`.

Status: **done. Gate met** on hardware, on the timed binaries, in 3 runs on both rocke nodes (n01-09 twice, n07-17
once; fresh seeds and allocations).
- Every positive protocol was clean in every launch.
- Every negative control and mutant failed as required.
- Timing followed DESIGN 9.1: shuffled rounds, two controls, the win rule, an unstamped metric next to the stamped
  one, and the node recorded per run.
- The 3 runs agree within about 0.03 on every ratio.

## 1. What D0 gets from P5 (summary)

1. **Coherence does not push the plan toward Path B.** Both hazards are real on gfx950, and they are total, not
   rare. The design's rules close both of them at a cost below 0.001 hybrid units per K=3 round. A kernel boundary
   is not needed as a coherence point. P5 is a correctness gate, not a lever. D0 still rests on P1 (hand-off cost
   in situ) and P4.
2. **KV rewrite-after-read across XCDs (the `round` draft forwards, `loop`):**
   - **Writer side: the hazard is total.** Plain stores published without `buffer_wbl2 sc1` were wrong for 100% of
     the reader-epochs on other XCDs, under every reader cache policy, `sc1` loads included. Same-XCD readers were
     fine. The dirty line stays in the writer XCD's L2.
   - **Reader side: staleness is mostly through the vL1D, and it is never zero.** A reader without
     `buffer_inv sc1` was wrong in 3.6% of the reader-epochs when the row stayed vL1D-resident (cross-XCD and
     same-XCD alike).
     - When the reader streamed 64 KiB between re-reads, enough to evict the vL1D, there were still 4 isolated
       stale 128-byte lines in 589,824 reader-epochs over 3 runs, all cross-XCD (one more in smoke2).
     - Whether those 4 came from vL1D residue or the L2 is not established. There is no systematic L2 staleness
       across XCDs.
     - So no reader is "safe by eviction": the acquire is mandatory.
   - **`buffer_inv sc0` is not a cheaper acquire.** It is a workgroup-scope no-op and fails exactly like no
     invalidate.
   - **Write-after-read order is required.** A writer that rewrote the row before the readers finished made 97.9% of
     the reader-epochs wrong. Path A's dataflow supplies this order: Q5 -> Q6 -> Q7 -> ... -> head(f) -> draft Q2(f+1).
     It holds as long as each reader consumes its loads before it publishes downstream, which tagged mailboxes
     guarantee.
3. **Recommended default for KV rows written in the launch: `bulk=sc1`.**
   - The protocol: `sc1` stores, `vmcnt(0)`, then the ready pair. Readers `sc1`-load only the rows this launch wrote
     (positions >= `L`, known to the reader) and plain-load the rest, with no invalidate.
   - It is endorsed by the memory model (agent-scope monotonic store and load) and moves the same data, so it is
     bitwise-neutral.
   - Cost: 0.62-0.82 of the bulk rule's hand-off (`hop`) and 0.78-0.87 of its unstamped per-epoch time. That is a
     WIN in all 18 cell-runs.
   - The absolute per-hand-off saving is in the private file. At the 2 in-launch draft-KV hand-offs of a K=3 round that is
     about 0.0004 hybrid units: the right default, not a budget line.
   - DESIGN section 4 lists `bulk=sc1` as a knob. P5 says it should be the default for these rows. The bulk rule
     stays the fallback for bulk data whose fresh part the reader cannot name.
4. **Scalar path: the C-B5 rule is mandatory.**
   - An `s_load_dword` or `s_buffer_load_dword` of a word rewritten in the same launch returned the first value the
     scalar cache saw, for the whole launch. That is 100% of re-reads, 98.4% of all CTA-epochs, on both nodes, for
     both writer protocols. A missed instance therefore fails Tier G every time instead of intermittently.
   - `buffer_inv sc1` does not touch the scalar cache. `s_dcache_inv` or `glc` loads read fresh values.
   - Cost relative to carrying the value in the ready pair (the design default, `reg` = 1):

     | read | `hop_med` | unstamped per epoch |
     |---|---|---|
     | `sc1` vector load | 1.15-1.16 | 1.06-1.09 |
     | `s_dcache_inv` + `s_load` | 1.15-1.16 | 1.03-1.09 |
     | `s_dcache_inv` + `buffer_inv sc1` + `s_load` (the memory-model-safe re-read) | 2.62-2.69 | 1.72-1.78 |
     | `s_load ... glc` | 3.8-4.0 | 3.4-3.5 |

   - `s_dcache_inv` + `s_load` alone was clean here (0 of 786,432 CTA-epochs). That relies on the L2 being
     read-coherent in this configuration (SPX/NPS1, `mtype_local` default), which the memory model does not promise.
     Avoid `glc`.
   - This also answers the rev-3 "scalar-memory poll on `lgkmcnt`" idea (DESIGN 12.6). A scalar poll of an
     in-launch word never sees the update unless every retry carries `glc` or `s_dcache_inv`. A `glc` read's
     hand-off is about 3.4x that of an `s_dcache_inv` read (its extra cost about 20x), so a scalar poll needs
     `s_dcache_inv` per retry (config-dependent) or it does not work. Measure it before building on it.
5. **Loop mode (input to WP12; extension, not in the gate):**
   - The test: the host rewrites device buffers between rounds of one launch, through `hipMemcpyAsync` H2D (SDMA,
     or blit with `HSA_ENABLE_SDMA=0`) or a writer kernel on another stream.
   - Plain vector re-reads go stale in about 100% of CTA-rounds through the vL1D. `buffer_inv sc1` (agent scope),
     `buffer_inv sc0 sc1`, `sc1` loads and `sc0 sc1` loads were all clean.
   - Scalar re-reads stayed fresh in every host variant. The dispatch experiment explains why: foreign dispatches
     and copies evidently invalidate the scalar caches of the running kernel's CUs. In-launch scalar re-reads went
     from 100% wrong to 16-18% with about 6,000 foreign 1-CTA dispatches during the launch, and to 0% with about
     15,000 2 KiB copies. The vL1D was untouched by either.
   - This is a side effect. WP12 keeps `s_dcache_inv`. Another consequence is that foreign work during a persistent
     launch costs it scalar-cache refills.
6. **K=0 `pass` has no in-launch KV re-read.** Each layer takes row `L` from the mailboxes (`qsa.py`: "nothing in
   this launch reads row L back"). The KV result matters for `round` (draft forwards), `loop` and any future in-launch
   re-read. The scalar result matters for every granularity above `layers=1`: WP5a's launch-start prologue/meta
   writes per-step words that later stages of the same launch use.

|---|---|

All new code is Python-engine only, uses the `q38p5_` kernel names (the family README's cpp-parity exemption) and is
imported by no served build. P4's `p4_sync.py` helpers are imported, not edited, so every existing build stays
byte-identical.

The only existing file touched is `library/tests/test_builder_signature_contract.py`, which gained 4 entries
registering the P5 specs.
- The P5 builders take `(spec, arch)` per the rocKE builder contract, so they do not appear among that test's
  failures. The test was already failing before P5, on the family's `framework`, `moe`, P2 and P4 builders.
- That signature edit came after the GPU runs. `hackathon-prep/qwen38/truemono/P5/ir_identity.py` shows the LLVM IR
  of all 126 builds is identical between the timed stage and the final tree.

**Kernel shapes**
- Grid and placement: 256 x 512 (KV) and 256 x 256 (scalar), with P4's LDS pad, so there is one CTA per CU (checked
  every run: 256 distinct CUs, xcc == cta % 8).
- KV writer: one writer CTA per epoch. Its XCD rotates through all 8 (`wtab`).
- KV rows: each tail row is a 1 KiB K row plus a 1 KiB V row (2 kv heads x 256 bf16, `[pages, 16, 2, 256]`). The tail
  is 1 row (r1 draft) or 4 rows (r4s), and is the same rows every epoch, as a rollback rewrites positions `L..L+k`.
- KV readers: 64 reader CTAs (the last 64, 8 per XCD; Q5 has 130 tasks and Q7 48). Each reads the tail plus a slice
  of 32 stable rows (Q5's 32-row tile) or of 1 row (the tail stays vL1D-resident).
  - `hot`: the same slice every epoch.
  - `cold`: slices walk a 64 MiB buffer, larger than any XCD's L2.
- Epochs end with a closing counter sync (P4's 8-stripe barrier, every CTA). It orders the next rewrite after the
  reads.
- Every word read is checked. Wrong words are classified as stale (the previous epoch's value) or other, and per
  reader CTA as same-XCD or cross-XCD relative to the writer.
- Scalar kernel: one writer CTA per epoch stores the table word (`buffer_wbl2 sc1` or a `sc1` store) and a ready pair
  carrying the same value. Wave 0 of every CTA polls the pair, then reads the word by mode. Every CTA read the word
  in the previous epoch, so a stale line is always there to be hit.
- Host kernel: 128 CTAs, a round word and per-CTA acks in pinned host memory (system scope). The host rewrites the
  table word and a 2 KiB block between rounds.

**Metrics** (`s_memrealtime`, one clock for all XCDs)
- `hop_max` / `hop_med`: last (median) reader done minus the writer's publish start. Median over epochs >= 2, per
  launch.
- `per_epoch`: launch span (first CTA start to last CTA end) / epochs. Measured on builds with no in-loop stamps
  (9.1's unstamped timing); it includes the closing sync, so its ratios are diluted toward 1.

## 5. Design consequences (proposed; for the D0 record and the WPs)

| where | P5 says |
|---|---|
| DESIGN 4, row "KV rows, index lists, compressed keys (bulk)" | for rows the reader can name as written in this launch (draft forwards' rows >= `L`), default `bulk=sc1` (`sc1` stores + `sc1` loads of those rows only, no `wbl2`/`inv`); the bulk rule stays for bulk data whose fresh part the reader cannot name. Never drop the writer-side write-back without `sc1` stores. `buffer_inv sc0` is never an acquire |
| DESIGN 4 / 7.5, in-launch words (C-B5) | confirmed mandatory: in-launch-written words go in registers or the mailbox payload; a scalar re-read of one is wrong for the whole launch. If a table must be re-read in-launch: `sc1` vector load (1.06-1.21x) or `s_dcache_inv` + `buffer_inv sc1` (memory-model-safe, 1.7-2.7x) |
| WP5a (launch-start prologue/meta) | every word the prologue writes must reach later stages in registers or mailboxes; the mono's `table_entry` (`s_buffer_load`) must never read a prologue-written table. P5's `smem`/`sbuf` controls become WP5a mutants |
| WP5b/WP5c (draft forwards in `round`) | `bulk=sc1` for the draft KV rows; the write-after-read order rides on Q5/Q6/Q7 -> head(f) -> Q2(f+1); P5's `plain` writer and `early` mutants become Tier G race mutants |
| WP12 (`loop`) | host-rewritten device buffers: `buffer_inv sc1` (system scope by the memory model) or `sc1`/`sc0 sc1` loads; keep `s_dcache_inv` for scalar re-reads even though foreign dispatches/copies refresh the scalar cache today |
| DESIGN 12.6 "scalar-memory poll on `lgkmcnt`" | a scalar poll of an in-launch word needs `glc` (slow) or `s_dcache_inv` per retry (fast, config-dependent): measure before building on it |

## 6. Caveats

- **Configuration.** Both nodes run SPX / NPS1 with `mtype_local` at the driver default (-1), on coarse-grained
  `hipMalloc` memory. The "no systematic L2 staleness across XCDs" and `smem_dinv` observations are facts about that
  configuration, not guarantees of the memory model; the 4 isolated stale lines in section 3 are a reason not to
  lean on them. The recommended defaults (`bulk=sc1`, register carry) are
  endorsed by the memory model and do not depend on them.
- **Microbenchmark.** There is no weight traffic, and every reader reads the tail. In the real draft forward only
  the Q5/Q7 tasks that cover rows >= `L` read them. The per-hand-off saving is small in absolute terms anyway
  (private file).
- **Write-after-read order.** It is modelled by a closing counter sync. In the kernel the dataflow provides it; the
  mutant shows what happens without it, not the cost of the dataflow edge.
- **Not covered by this harness.** `sc1` load bandwidth for large in-launch blocks; P5's rows are 2 KiB each, and
  `bulk=sc1` assumes the fresh part is small.
- **Neighbours.** full1 started with one other job of ours on the node (`w3-atom-tp1`); full2 and full3 had none.
  The ratios of all three runs agree within 0.03.

  numerics. Section 5's mutant list rightly leaves it out, so WP5b/WP5c/WP12 need a **static ISA/IR check** instead:
  every load of a launch-written KV row must carry `sc1` or follow `buffer_inv sc1`. Do not count on a numerics
  gate for this.
- **"Endorsed by the memory model" overstates `bulk=sc1`.**

  - Why that is not formally a release/acquire pair: the model's agent-scope release adds `buffer_wbl2 sc1`, and its
    acquire adds `buffer_inv sc1`.
  - Why it is still sound on this hardware: every line is agent-coherent and completion is awaited. P5's hardware
    result agrees: `sc1/sc1` was clean, while `plain` and `none` failed.
  - Suggested wording: "agent-scope atomic encodings, ordered by `vmcnt(0)` and the poll dependency". Keep the
    `plain` writer and the reader-without-`sc1` checks as guards.
- The loop-mode conclusion that foreign dispatches refresh scalar caches is an inferred mechanism, supported by the
