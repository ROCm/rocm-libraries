(the attention and MLP HC norm + down-projection + injection) over more CTAs in the FlyDSL mono copy
(`dev/ATOM-truemono`), variants (i) 80 units with A2-side sums, (ii) 20 one-n-block CTAs with the in-CTA ordered
reduce, (iii) (ii) with the request rows deduplicated; r1 and r4s on `best`; bitwise vs the mono; layer A/B with both
controls. This file gives ratios, shares and counts only. Absolute times:
`hackathon-prep/private-perf/qwen38-true-mono/probes/P3.md`.

Status: **done, gate met.** Every variant is bitwise vs the plain mono on hardware (9 layer cells, 3 kinds, rows 1-4),
deterministic, with must-fail mutants that all fail; the off state is ISA-identical (24 builds). Timing per DESIGN 9.1:
five jobs, both rocke nodes, five seeds, 40 shuffled rounds per process, two controls, unstamped builds. The three
n07-17 jobs had at most one busy job of ours beside them; the two n01-09 jobs had three to four (the neighbour rule of

## 5. Why (stamped twins, gpu1, n07-17; stage ends as shares of the stamped `best` launch)

Stamped builds bias timing (DESIGN 9.1); these are read for structure only, never as the result.

- **A1's time is its per-CTA norm, not its weight bytes.** delta r1: A1 ends at 0.176 of the launch (`best`); one A1
  task lasts 0.137. Spreading to 84 units cuts the task to 0.093 and A1's end to 0.153 (A2's end 0.204 -> 0.173), and
  the gain carries to the layer end (0.968). What is left in each unit is the kernel entry, the `h` and `w'` loads, the
  square sums and inverse RMS over all 10240 values, and the pack. The quarter pack removes three quarters of the pack,
  which is the extra 0.4-0.7 % of value 10 over value 1. The rest (statistics over the whole row on every CTA) needs the
  norm statistics from the producer (P3x v: norm after GEMV, or M5's per-tile square sums on `xl`).
- **Dedupe moves the per-row norm onto fewer CTAs.** delta r4s: A1's end 0.112 (`best`, 56 one-row tasks) -> 0.143
  ((iii), 20 four-row tasks) and M1's end 0.499 -> 0.572; layer 1.109 stamped, 1.121 unstamped.
- **M1 spread: M1 itself gets no slower, the MLP tail does.** delta r1, both HCs (i): M1 ends earlier (0.620 vs
  0.643) but the layer ends at 0.991, against 0.968 with A1 alone. M1-only: the layer ends 0.033 later, most of it
  between M4's end and M5's end. The MLP half has no free CTAs: C1 holds 160, M2 80, M3 65, M4 110 and M5 160 of 256,
  and every placement of 24-84 M1 tasks puts them in front of M2, M3, M4 or M5 work on the same CTA. Keeping M2 / M3 at
  the mono's bases (`_k`) is worst (M1 and M2 share CTAs and serialize). A1's extra CTAs only sit in front of B1 tasks,
  which wait for A2 anyway.
- r4s: A1 ends 0.019 earlier with (ii) (stamped), but the unstamped delta layer does not move (0.998): at rows 4 the
  layer's critical path leaves A1 less exposed than at r1.

## 6. Projection method (`p3_summary.py project`)

Per cell, the median over jobs of the booked arm's median ratio; per-layer weights = the base arm's median per-launch
time (private absolute, per cell). K=0: 0.894 x (sum over delta r1 x 36 and qsa r1 x 12 of time x (1 - ratio)) / (sum
of time). K=3: 0.775 x the same over delta r4s x 36 and qsa r4s x 12, plus 0.047 x the same over qsa r4s x 1 and
qsa r1 x 2 (the drafter's QSA layer at r4s, r1, r1). Booked: r1 `spread.a1=10`, qsa r4s `spread=2`, delta r4s nothing. The n07-17 jobs alone give the same values (K=0 0.028, K=3 0.004).
Caveats: the FlyDSL `best` layers here run the FlyDSL MoE and B3, the hybrid runs the rocKE ones (shorter layers, so
A1 is a slightly larger share; the projection errs low); delta_ple booked as delta (not timed: 1 of 48 layers, bitwise
checked).

## 7. Not done, and why

- Polled-input quarter pack (delta_ple's A1 polls P2; M1 polls C1): that path would need a second poll in the pack's
  layout, and both are already measured as not worth it (1 of 48 layers; M1 does not spread).
- Half units (2 partials per CTA, so (i) fits 4 rows: 176 tasks): not built. At r4s the A1 spreads that exist ((ii),
  dedupe) are inside the controls on delta, so the expected gain is below the 0.002 resolution there. Worth a try only
  if WP2a's rocKE A1 at r4s turns out tail-bound like r1.
- P1's in-kernel `xl` (Path A) with the spread on: P1's arms, not built here (1.4).
- Served e2e: no. P3 is a probe of a layer lever. The hybrid-adoptable knob goes to the tuner's lookback pool
  (`spread.a1=10` at r1, `spread=2` at qsa r4s), as every retained knob does.

## Verification (independent verifier, W2 vP3, 2026-10-31)

Verdict: **confirmed**, with the caveats below. Neither the result nor the gate is refuted. Every gate in DESIGN 8.2's
P3 row was actually exercised on hardware: bitwise vs the mono, mutants that must fail, a layer A/B with both
controls, and the K=0 kill criterion. Absolute numbers for this section are in `private-perf/.../probes/P3-raw/w2-vP3-s10a/`.

**What I checked, from the code and the raw logs (not from the summary):**
- **The numerics really ran on hardware and the mutants really fail.** I read `job.out` / `CHECK` lines of gpu1-gpu6.
  - Every arm is bitwise and deterministic, and its layer output is equal unmasked, in all 9 cells.
  - `drop6` / `drop7` / `drop8` / `drop9` / `q11` / `c1` differ in `h_out` and in the states (`rec`, `conv`), not
    only in masked scratch. One example, delta r1 `drop7`: 27248 `h_out` bytes differ.
  - The rig cannot pass on stale data. `LayerRig` zeroes the scratch before the snapshot, every `run_once` restores
    that snapshot and the counter, and the reference is the plain `mono` build, not `best`. A spread build that skips
    a mailbox write would therefore read zeros, not the mono's values. The mask covers only `lr8` / `lre` / `mlr8` /
    `mlre` and the spread words, for the arm and its mutants alike.
- **The env reaches the kernel.** Spread arms give different layouts (A1 84 / 24 tasks), different timings and
  differing mutants, so `ATOM_Q38_SCHED` is not silently ignored.
- **The `q11` "failure" on delta_ple r1 is by construction, not a hole.** `stages/hc.py:828` sets
  `quarter = sv in (10, 11) and not hc.polled and nr == 1`. On the polled PLE A1, values 10 and 11 compile to value
  1's code. That path is bitwise there, and `drop7` fails there. `q11` differs in the 4 memory-read cells (delta
  r1, qsa r1, delta r3s, qsa r2s) in both gpu5 and gpu6.
  - Tooling nit: the harness counts `q11` as must-fail on polled HCs, so gpu5 and gpu6 exit with status 1. It should
    be marked n/a on polled HCs.
- **Off = ISA-identical.** `orig/` (`sched.py`, `layout.py`, `hc.py`) is byte-equal to `dev/ATOM`'s files, so the
  "pre" tree is the real baseline.
  - cpu1 and cpu2 both report `KEYS_CMP off-plain` / `off-best` as 12/12 PASS and `mutant-spread` as FAIL, as it must.
  - cpu1 (the earlier tree) had `MAT FAIL` and 2 CPU tests failing with "No gfx arch". cpu2 (the final tree)
    supersedes it: MAT PASS (83 builds, the only scratch is the 68-byte spill that `best` qsa r4s already has) and 34
    tests pass.
- **The timing and the final tree match.** gpu2-gpu4 ran on the earlier ATOM stage (`atom-truemono-w2-p3`, values
  1-9); gpu5 and gpu6 ran on the final one (`-p3b`, synced after the last edits).
  - I compared the compile matrices. For every value-1, -2 and -4 build, and every value-3 build but one, ELF and
    `.text` sha are identical between cpu1 (old tree) and cpu2 (final tree). The exception is value 3 on
    delta_ple r4s, which is not booked.
  - So the old-tree timings of (i) / (ii), including the booked qsa r4s `spread=2`, describe the final tree.
- **Timing discipline (9.1) holds.** `p3_rig.timed_rounds` shuffles the order per round with a seed and computes one
  ratio per round vs `best`. The controls are `best2` (a second graph) and `best_rc` (`best;pause=0`, a separate
  compile). `_gate` is exactly 9.1's WIN rule: median below both controls' minimum by 0.002, and at least 3/4 of the
  rounds below 1.
  - Every table in section 4 matches the `#####` blocks of the job logs.
  - The ref gates are narrow but pass. `S10a/S1a` 0.9954 against a threshold of 0.9955 (gpu5) and 0.9958 against
    0.9961 (gpu6).

1. **The kill-criterion margin is about the size of the method error.** The booked 0.027 applies the FlyDSL-rig layer
   ratio to the hybrid's 0.894 layer share. The `best` rig layers here are 1.03-1.07 of the hybrid's layer time (the
