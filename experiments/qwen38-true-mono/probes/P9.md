# Probe P9: the MTP round tail as one persistent launch with real work (DESIGN 8.2)

Status: done 2026-10-31 (W2). The numerics gate passes on hardware, bitwise against ATOM's ops, for every build
reported here. Mutants were run, and the ones that slip past the gate are listed. Timing follows DESIGN 9.1:
3 processes x 20 shuffled interleaved rounds x 16 chains per arm, 4 cold weight sets, two A-vs-A controls per
comparison, unstamped builds, node recorded. This file holds ratios only. Absolute times are in
`hackathon-prep/private-perf/qwen38-true-mono/probes/P9.md`, with raw files in `P9-raw/`. A ratio below 1 means
faster. "hu" = hybrid units, i.e. shares of the hybrid K=3 CONC 1 round.

## The question, and the answer D0 needs

P9 asks: what does the true mono gain by running the non-layer work between two layer passes of a K=3 round as
stages of one persistent launch, instead of ATOM's kernel sequence? That work is the final HC mix, the MXFP4 head
with the greedy argmax, the accept, the drafter's front (embedding norm + `fc_embedding` + hidden norm + `fc_hidden`)
and the draft meta. ATOM's sequence is about 20 kernels for the verify tail and 7-10 for each draft tail.

1. **The in-kernel round tail wins, but only after restructuring. A straight port loses.**
   - The first gated build (`lat=v1`, the straight port) lost on every comparison: verify chain 1.094-1.126
     (LOSS 3/3), draft chains 1.005-1.078.
   - The final build (`best_spec`) wins on every comparison, 3/3 processes (job 68231718; the separate lever job
     68231745 reproduces it within 0.02):

     | chain | true mono / hybrid | verdict |
     |---|---|---|
     | verify | **0.795 [0.789-0.798]** | WIN 3/3 |
     | draft step 0 -> 1 (4 rows in) | **0.848 [0.842-0.862]** | WIN 3/3 |
     | later draft steps (1 row) | **0.890 [0.879-0.891]** | WIN 3/3 |
     | the same three, eager (busy-ahead) | 0.795 / 0.843 / 0.886 | WIN 3/3 each |

2. **G3 measured: about 0.018 hu at K=3 CONC 1, ratio method, booked.** Each chain's measured ratio is applied to
   its segment of the served K=3 round trace:

   | segment | share of round | x (1 - ratio) | hu |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | verify tail | 0.0414 | 0.205 | 0.0085 |
   | draft tail 1 | 0.0308 | 0.152 | 0.0047 |
   | draft tail 2 | 0.0272 | 0.110 | 0.0030 |
   | last draft step's mix + head + sample | 0.0199 | 0.110 | 0.0022 |

   - **Upper bound, not booked: about 0.028 hu.** This uses absolute differences against the served segments, which
     also hold copies and launches the bench chains do not model, after removing the gap between the served head
     kernel and the bench's head.
   - **Not covered by P9:** the next round's prologue (copies, meta, input prep: 0.022 of the round).
   - **Against the booking:** DESIGN 2.2 books G3 at K=3 at 0.03-0.065 for Path A and 0.015-0.045 for Path B (the
     tail launch). P9's four tails, 0.018 booked and about 0.028 at most, sit inside Path B's band and below Path A's.
     Path A reaches its band only if the unmeasured next-round prologue (0.022 of the round) mostly vanishes too.
     Re-issue G3 at about 0.02 measured, plus the prologue as booked.
   - **Overlap with P2:** P2's target-head fused-argmax gain (0.002 hu) is inside the verify-chain number. Do not add
     it twice.

3. **P9 is Path B's tail launch, measured. Its gain is also hybrid-adoptable, and D0 must weigh that.**
   - P9 is a standalone launch whose input is the last layer's streams in memory. That is the structure DESIGN 2.2
     gives Path B's tail launch, so P9 measures Path B's G3 directly.
   - The same launch could replace ATOM's tail sequence in today's hybrid, without the layer stack in the same
     launch.
   - The part only Path A gets is the last-layer -> MIX hop inside the layer launch (G1 / P1 territory). P9 does not
     measure it.

4. **What WP2e must carry (the levers that made the difference).** Each is measured below, and all are knobs in
   `p9_tail.py`:
   - **Issue every independent load before the first use; poll each mailbox row once per row-wave** (`lat=v2w`).
     The v1 PRE / MIX was load-use chains behind `scf_if` store regions, plus four waves each polling every row's
     1,280 parts, one row after another. That took the head's x arrival to 0.47 of the chain; v2w brings it to 0.29.
   - **One epoch arrival per CTA, not per wave** (`ectr=cta`): lever 0.884-0.910 by itself. Each of 2,048 waves
     adding 1 to the launch counter meant about 430 idle waves hitting one address at launch start. That stalled the
     early loads and the mailbox traffic in the same scratch buffer. Book this in WP1's platform: per-CTA (or
     per-XCD) arrivals.
   - **Deliver the argmax directly** (`fold=direct`). Each head CTA puts its own (key, column) per row, and every
     FRONT task folds the 243 slots and runs the accept itself. This removes the last-arriver atomic, slot poll and
     ids hop.
   - **Normalize the FRONT embedding rows once into LDS, not inside the MFMA chain** (`fnorm=pre`). The post-accept
     tail drops from 0.07 to 0.02 of the chain.
   - **Hold the head's first-panel prefetch on the verify chain** (`hdelay=8`). The head waits for x much longer than
     its prefetch takes, and the prefetch burst delays the PRE / MIX loads: verify lever 0.970-0.979, but the drafts
     lose 0.6-2.0%. So it is verify-only.

5. **Hazards found that WP1 / WP2e must carry** (details under Findings):
   - a read-after-write race through ATOM's aliased `input_ids` buffer once the accept is no longer one wave;
   - per-wave epoch atomics;
   - `s_barrier` across wave groups;
   - the sc1 poll flood;
   - SGPR pressure surfacing as a fault;
   - LDS reads consumed before they land (`_lds_settle`);
   - three mutants the output gate cannot see.

What the final timelines say is left:

- **The head's weight stream is about 0.6 of the draft chain and 0.5 of the verify chain.** That is parity with the
  hybrid head kernel.
- **Two mailbox hops of about 0.03-0.05 each:** PRE -> MIX, and MIX -> head (hint + payload).
- **MIX compute is latency-bound: about 0.03 of the chain per row.** It does not respond to pure asm guards (`opq`),

  the end of the head loop, is the suspect; this was not isolated further.

## Findings

1. **A read-after-write race through ATOM's `input_ids` buffer.** The verify accept reads the step's draft ids at
   `input_ids[1..3]` and scatters the next token into the drafter's ids at `input_ids[1..4]`: the same buffer, offset
   by one element.
   - Under `fold=direct`, every FRONT task runs the accept and task 0 writes the outputs. A task that read the draft
     ids after task 0's scatter took the new ids as drafts.
   - The failures appeared only on tasks 8, 24, 40, 64, ...: CTAs on the same XCD as CTA 0, which see its plain
     stores in their shared L2 first.
   - Fix: every task reads both id sets before its MIX publishes and pins them (`fresh` asm). No task can be past
     its MIX before every head CTA has finished, and task 0 writes only after all of them.
   - WP2e: any accept that is not one wave must read its inputs before the stage that releases the consumers.
2. **Per-wave epoch arrivals were a hot spot** (`ectr`, lever 0.88-0.91). Every wave adding 1 to one 64-bit counter
   at its end meant about 430 atomics at launch start, from the waves with no work, plus the rest at their ends.
   Moving to the CTA's last wave (an LDS counter, then one add of 8) sped up the early loads and the mailboxes in the
   same buffer. WP1's ticket / epoch counters should count CTAs, or use per-XCD counters.
3. **Polls must be throttled.** 243 head CTAs spinning on sc1 loads starved the 160 producer CTAs: hangs and
   timeouts in the first builds. Fixed by a wave-0 hint poll with the other waves at a barrier, `s_sleep 1` per
   retry, and one wave polling the ids and broadcasting them through LDS (`xwait`, `pause`, `idwait`).
4. **`s_barrier` counts every wave of the workgroup.** With the head on waves 0-3 and tasks on 4-7, a group barrier
   is an LDS counter per site, zeroed at entry behind the only hardware barrier (`Bars`).
5. **LDS reads were consumed before they landed (observed, root cause not proven).** A deterministic 1-ulp mismatch
   in an f32 sum of LDS-exchanged partials appeared when the compiler consumed mixed-width `ds_read` results under
   partial `lgkmcnt(N)` waits. An asm `s_waitcnt lgkmcnt(0)` that takes every loaded value as a tied operand
   (`_lds_settle`) removed it, and so did a second barrier.
6. **SGPR pressure appeared as a fault, not a spill warning.** The first verify builds faulted with 23 SGPR spills.
   Building buffer resources and mailbox bases at each use, and dropping `readfirstlane` on uniform values, fixed
   it. The final verify build is at the 256-VGPR limit with 11 spills. A per-row PRE store region (`prer=row`)
   raised that to 38, and its stamped build hung twice (cause not found; the knob is not used).
7. **The head's first-panel prefetch competes with the producers' loads.** P2 saw this too. Holding it (`hdelay`)
   brought the first PRE loads in 2-3x sooner. It pays on the verify chain, where the head waits for x longest.
8. **Defensive pin.** With `espec`, the FRONT stages the speculative ids' rows and later re-stages the rows whose id
   changed. In the v2 builds the speculative ids are pinned (`fresh`), so the staging and the re-stage test use one
   value even if the compiler re-materializes the load.
9. **Masked mutants** (`sumsq_order`, `en_order`, draft `hn_order`): see the gate. Output-bitwise gates do not
   cover re-association inside a norm that ends in a bf16 rounding.

## Verification (independent verifier, W2, 2026-10-31)

DESIGN 9.1 and reproduces on the other rocke node with a fresh seed. The G3 arithmetic and trace shares reproduce.
The caveats and the corrections to the text above are listed below. Nothing here changes the D0 inputs: P9 wins
on all three chains, and G3 at K=3 CONC 1 (greedy) is about 0.018 hu booked, about 0.028 at most.

What was checked:

- **The gate on hardware (job logs re-read).** Log `w2-p9-g2-68231711` (n01-09): final `best_spec` builds TV 96,

2. **Throttled rounds are not dropped.**

     does not track the job's GPU, and `summarize_p9.py` does not use it.
   - The ratios are in-process and interleaved, so a clock drift hits both arms.
3. **The eager comparisons are judged against the graph controls.** There is no eager A-vs-A arm. Their per-round
   spread is tight (about ±0.01), but "WIN" on the eager rows is not controlled in the 9.1 sense.
4. **The masked mutants are four builds, not three, and the case counts are lower than stated.**

     emulation of ATOM's order, so these mutants fail.

     0.795 does not carry over to them.
