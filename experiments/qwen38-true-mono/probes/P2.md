# Probe P2: the lm_head as a stage of a persistent launch (DESIGN 8.2)

Status: done 2026-10-30 (W2). Gate PASS on hardware on both rocke nodes; timing per DESIGN 9.1 on n07-17
(3 processes x 30 shuffled rounds x 128 launches per arm, two A-vs-A controls per baseline). Ratios only here;
absolute times in `hackathon-prep/private-perf/qwen38-true-mono/probes/P2.md` (raw files in `P2-raw/`).
Below 1 is faster. "hu" = hybrid units (the hybrid round at K=3, the hybrid step at K=0).

## The question, and the answer D0 needs

P2 asks what the true mono gains or pays by running the MXFP4 head (`mx_decode_gemv`, bf16 activations) as a stage
of a persistent launch, behind a producer stage, with the head panels issued before the poll and the argmax fused,
instead of as its own graph-launched kernel after the kernel that produced its input (the hybrid).

1. **G4 (head in the kernel: boundary + ramp) is about 0, not 0.002-0.006 hu.** One launch (producer stage + head
   stage) against the hybrid's two graph-launched kernels at the same epilogue: 0.997 (rows 1) and 1.000 (rows 4) at
   no drain, 1.001 / 0.995 with a producer drain of about 0.04 of the chain; every cell NEUTRAL in 3 of 3 processes. The in-kernel
   producer -> head hop (sc1 mailbox, cross-XCD) costs about what a graph boundary plus re-staging x costs; stamps
   (below) show why. Converted: draft heads at K=3 gain 0.0001 hu in total. **Book G4 at 0** (Path A and B alike)
   unless a hop is removed (see 7).
2. **The head-side gain that is real is the fused argmax replacing ATOM's logits + greedy sampler on the TARGET
   head**: 0.889 (rows 4) / 0.926 (rows 1) of the hybrid's target-head chain (logits kernel + `topk_select`), WIN
   3/3 at every rows and delay; bitwise equal to ATOM's sampler with `nanrule="sampler"`. That is **0.0020-0.0021 hu
   at K=3 CONC 1 and 0.0021-0.0022 hu at K=0 CONC 1**, and 0.85-0.89 of the hybrid target head at 8-16 rows (CONC
   2-4). It belongs to G3 (sampling), not G4, and **it is hybrid-adoptable** (the hybrid could fuse greedy rows into
   its rocKE head too: DESIGN 2.6), so it is not a structural margin.
3. **The head stage must run on 4 waves of a 512-thread CTA**: head-only parity with the served head at 4 waves
   (1.000 at 256 threads, 1.002 at 512 threads with waves 4-7 idle), **8 waves lose 4.3-4.8%** (LOSS 3/3). Waves 4-7
   are free during the head (but see 6: using them to prefetch the head's own panels into L2 loses).
4. **Issuing the head panels before the poll is mandatory, not a lever to gain**: without it the in-kernel chain
   LOSES 1.6-2.4% to the two-launch hybrid (rows 1-4); with it, parity. Lever ratio 0.975-0.981 (rows 1-4), 0.968-0.984
   with a drain, 0.992-0.995 at rows 8-16 without a drain.
5. **x hand-off mode is a per-width choice**: tagged bf16-pair mailbox (`pairs`) at rows <= 4 (the mono bulk rule
   loses 1.0-3.5% there); bulk rule at rows >= 8 (bulk beats pairs by 1.7-2.8% of the chain at 8 and 16 rows: pairs
   carry twice the bytes through sc1 loads on 243 CTAs).
6. **Losers (retained-off knobs)**: spare-wave L2 prefetch of the head waves' next load groups (`l2pf` 2 / 4 / 8):
   1.004-1.067 without a drain (LOSS except `l2pf=2` at rows 8-16), 0.991-1.063 with one, never a WIN: the prefetch
   loads delay the x hand-off itself (stamps). Head
   on 8 waves (`waves=8`): 1.03-1.05. In-kernel full logits (`logits=out`, WP8 phase 1) with `pairs`: parity at rows
   1-4, 1.02-1.06 at 8-16 (use bulk there; not measured).
7. **What would make the head stage win structurally** (for WP2e / P9, not built here): (a) remove a hop: the
   head CTAs compute the final HC mix themselves from the `xl` streams they poll anyway, so the chain is last-layer
   M5 -> head with one hop instead of M5 -> mix -> head; (b) replace the last-arriver argmax fold, which is 0.054-0.061
   of a head chain at rows 1-4 and 0.112-0.123 at rows 16 (stamps), by per-CTA keys delivered straight to the
   consuming stage (accept / draft front) through a mailbox: one round trip instead of atomic + slot poll + store +
   consumer poll; (c) NUMA-local x copies (P4) for the remaining hop. Each is worth more than G4 as booked.
8. **Hazards found that WP1/WP2e must carry**: `tile.tagged_poll` can compile to a spin that never reloads (section
   "Findings"); ATOM's sampler and the mono argmax disagree on negative NaN; an in-kernel tag derived from the head's
   own arrival counter works without a host step word, but a broken tag invariant hangs (mutant) instead of failing,
   so polls need the ticket-style timeout (DESIGN 4, P4) before WP1's race-stress gates.

The launch epoch (tag of the x mailbox) is read at entry from the fused fold's never-reset 64-bit arrival counter:
launch e reads a count in [eG, eG + G) because no head CTA arrives before it has consumed x, so graph replays need
no host-written step word (logits builds add the same arrival). The producer issues its x loads before it waits for
the tag (one round trip), as the true mono's producer would hold its tag in a register.

Hybrid arms (the baselines): `HA` = ATOM's served argmax head (fused, `waves_per_eu=1`, rows_max 4); `HL` = served
logits head (rows_max 4 or 16) + ATOM's greedy sampler (`aiter.topk_select(logits, 1, tie="low")`); `CHA` / `CHL` =
the producer launch then `HA` / `HL` in one graph. Controls: `_ctl` (second graph of the same kernels) and `_rc` (the
same code object loaded as a second module). True-mono arms: `TA_*` fused argmax, `TL_*` logits; `none` = no producer
(x staged from memory: isolates grid / threads), `pairs` / `bulk` = with the producer stage; `256` / `512w4` / `512w8` =
threads per CTA and head waves; `after` = poll before issuing panels; `san` = `nanrule="sampler"`; `pfN` = `l2pf=N`.

## Findings during the build

1. **`tile.tagged_poll` hazard (rocKE platform, latent in served code).** Its lowering puts only
   `call void asm sideeffect "s_nop 0", ""()` (no memory clobber) in the retry block and the polled loads are readonly
   buffer intrinsics, so LLVM may hoist them out of the loop: the first `bulk` build compiled the ready-pair poll to one
   load (narrowed to the tag dword) followed by an endless `s_nop` / branch, and every `pairs` / `bulk` build hung on
   the first launch whose pair was not ready at the first read. `mx_decode_gemv`'s fused fold keeps its loads inside
   the loop in its current builds (verified in the disassembly), so the served head is not affected today, but nothing
   guarantees it. P2 now polls with `rocke.helpers.mailbox.poll` (opaque asm loop, `vmcnt(0)` per retry; knob
   `pimpl="asm"`, default) and keeps `pimpl="ir"` as a knob. Fix for the platform (not done here: it changes the IR
   bytes of every build that uses the op): a `~{memory}` clobber on the pause asm, or volatile loads.
2. **ATOM's greedy sampler and the mono argmax disagree on negative NaN** (`bench_p2.py rule`, planted bf16 rows):
   ATOM (`topk_select(tie="low")`, also `torch.argmax`) ranks a NaN of either sign above everything; the mono /
   `mx_decode_gemv` key ranks -NaN below -inf. Ties, +0 / -0 and infinities order the same. MXFP4 heads produce -NaN
   from inf x 0 products, so rows with a non-finite activation differ (in the gate 26 of 117 such mono-rule cells
   matched the sampler). The hybrid uses both rules today (target heads: sampler; draft heads: mono). `nanrule` lets
   each in-kernel head instance match the path it replaces bitwise (one compare + select per key; timing 1.000-1.004
   of the mono-rule build).
3. **A stale tag hangs instead of failing**: the stale-tag mutant breaks the epoch invariant (heads finish before the
   producers read the counter), so later launches poll for a tag nobody writes. Race-stress gates (DESIGN 7.8, 5) need
   a poll timeout that writes an error word.
4. **Rows-16 logits bimodality is per job / node here**: the r16 logits head ran ~1.14x slower in every process of
   both n07-17 jobs than in the n01-09 job; the fused r16 build moved < 1%. All rows-16 comparisons above are paired
   inside one process (the in-kernel logits build tracks the served one within 0.2% in every process).

## Verification (independent verifier, W2, 2026-10-30)

Verdict: **CONFIRMED, with corrections** (below). The gate is the DESIGN 8.2 P2 gate (argmax / logits bitwise vs
`mx_decode_gemv` + ATOM's sampler tie rule), it ran on hardware in the same job as the timing, and every gate class
now has a mutant that fails with a wrong value on hardware. The conclusions follow from the data.

What was checked:

- **Job logs** (`q38/logs/w2-p2-t2-68218146.out`): hang guard (7 variants), rule probe, gate `150/150 cells, spills 0
  -> GATE PASS`, the three mutants, then 3 timed processes (190 arms each, post-capture consistency PASS), the
  summary, the stamps; node n07-17; status 0. The raw `check.json` agrees: 150 cells, 3,456 stress launches with 0
  failures, 252 / 252 planted ties real and resolved low, 9 / 9 `nanrule="sampler"` special cells equal to the
  sampler, no spills. The `tie_high` mutant fails all 4 planted ties with wrong values (stress 0 / 24: random data
  rarely ties). The `rule` probe output matches finding 2.
- **Timing tables**: every ratio and verdict quoted above matches `P2-raw/t2/time/ratios.md` (3 processes x 30 rounds
  x 128 launches). The harness shuffles the arm order every round, times one graph replay per arm with a sync
  before it, has two controls per hybrid baseline (second graph `_ctl`, recompiled and separately loaded `_rc`), and
  `summarize_p2.py` applies the 9.1 WIN rule exactly (median below both controls' minima by 0.002 AND >= 3/4 rounds
  below 1; LOSS mirrored). Timing is unstamped (stamped builds only in the decomposition table). The four weight
  copies are about 5x the MALL in total, rotated every launch.
- **Hybrid-unit conversion** recomputed from the private absolute medians and the stated method: target-head fused
  argmax 0.0020 hu (K=3) and 0.0021 hu (K=0). Matches the text.
- **Baseline fidelity**: ATOM's all-greedy path is `topk_select(logits, 1, tie="low")` (`atom/model_ops/sampler.py`,
  the `all_greedy` branch), the op the `HL` / `CHL` arms time. The G3 line applies to greedy rows only.
- **CPU tests**: `test_qwen38_probe_p2.py` 102 passed (local, CPU). The rocKE tree has no modified tracked files; P2
  only adds new files (`qwen38_mono/probes/`, the new test).

Corrections and gaps found (none reverses a conclusion):

1. **The stale-x gate was not exercised by the builder.** Both `stale_tag` mutants only HUNG (the log has no wrong
   value; `mutant_MUT_stale_tag_*.jsonl` are empty). A hang shows the poll can deadlock, not that the stress
   predicate catches a consumer that reads the previous launch's x, which is the race this hand-off must exclude.
   **Closed by the verifier** (`hackathon-prep/qwen38/truemono/P2-verify/vp2.py`, jobs 68218367 and 68218459,
   n01-09). Each trial zeroes the arrival counter and the mailbox, runs one launch of the good build with x1, then one
   launch with a fresh x2 sharing those buffers, and judges the second launch with `check_build`'s stress predicate.
   With the `stale_tag` mutant as the second launch, `bulk` (producer delay 20) and `pairs` (delay 400) FAIL the
   predicate in 12 / 12 trials each (rows 4 and 1), and the output equals the rule over x1 in 12 / 12, so the
   mutant read stale x and the gate caught it. With a separately loaded good build as the second launch (control),
   both modes PASS 12 / 12. The `pairs` mutant at delay 20 hung again (its producer's next-tag stores overwrite the
   pairs that the stale poll waits for). This supports finding 3: polls need a timeout / error word.
2. **"Same result on n01-09" overstated the coverage.** The n01-09 gate (68217754) was 105 / 105 cells over 22 builds.
   The 10 `l2pf` / `bulk512w4` builds were gated only on n07-17. **Closed by the verifier**: those 10 builds plus the
   producer cells through `bench_p2.py check --only 512w4pf,bulk512w4` on n01-09 (job 68218367): 51 / 51 PASS, 0
   spills.
3. **Smaller deviations from DESIGN 9.1 (they do not change any verdict):**
   - (a) Lever rows (panels before poll, `l2pf`) have one control (the `_rc` copy), not two.
   - (b) Clocks were logged but no rounds were dropped. During timing the GPU sat at its power limit, with SCLK
     2.21-2.31 GHz throughout and no outlier window. Arms are shuffled per round, so this affects all arms alike.
