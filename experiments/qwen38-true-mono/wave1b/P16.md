# P16: the K=0 hop-cut probe, in situ on the tuned best base (gfx950)

D0 section 7.2 blocker B1: the K=0 headline target (<= 0.78 hu per token) has no lever program, and the adversarial
check guessed that each serial hand-off hop of a delta layer is worth about 0.025-0.029 hu, so 3.5-4 hop cuts (or
Path A plus 1-2) would close the gap. P16 measures what every hop of the K=0 layers (delta r1, delta_ple r1, qsa r1)
is worth, builds the cheapest hop cuts as default-off knobs in the FlyDSL copy (ATOM-truemono), proves them bitwise on
hardware with must-fail mutants, times them in situ on the served stack's layers on both rocke nodes, runs the winner
end to end at K=0 against the hybrid and the best stack, and turns the result into a K=0 lever program (section 7,
proposed for D0 sections 4 / 5). Ratios and hybrid units (hu: the pinned hybrid's K=0 step = 1) only; absolute times,
round trips and tok/s are in `hackathon-prep/private-perf/qwen38-true-mono/wave1b/P16.md`.

Status: **done, gate met for what was built.** Every lever arm is bitwise against the plain mono layer on hardware
(layer output and every written state, twice), every must-fail mutant differs, the off state is ISA-identical
(32 / 32 builds, three envs, two CPU jobs; one known rule-137 flake explained in 2), ATOM's own schedule-knob tests pass
(29 / 29, GPU job) on the final tree. Timing per DESIGN 9.1 in 6 GPU jobs (4 on n07-17, 2 on n01-09; 16 shuffled rounds,
two to five controls). The 8.1 neighbour rule held for g1 only (section 8; the n07-17 e2e job e1 did NOT meet it,
corrected in the fix round); the other jobs had 2-6 neighbours of ours, and their verdicts agree with g1 within the
controls. Verified PARTIAL by an independent W9 verifier (appended); the fix round (end of file) corrects the record
and re-runs the layer timing and the e2e under the 8.1 rule.

## 6. The cuts that were not built, and why

- **A1 -> A2 and M1 -> M2 fusion (consumer-side redundancy).** Every A2 (M2) task needs all 320 low-rank values, i.e.
  the whole down projection: about 3.3 MB of MXFP8 weights per consumer CTA, 80 consumers, about 260 MB of L2 / MALL
  reads per layer per HC for a hop worth 0.005 (A1 -> A2) / 0.012 (M1 -> M2) hu per D. The spread (P3) already does
  the affordable part: A2 sums the 80 units' 4 partials itself (one hop, no reducer).
- **N2 (C1 into B3 / Q7 epilogues).** The oracle bounds it at 0.026 hu at K=0 (C1's chain and weight stream gone,
  nothing added). As specified, each rocKE B3 task (96 at split 2) would multiply its 64 output rows by a 2560 x 64
  MXFP4 `W_o` slice and publish 2560 f32 partials: about 2 MB of `sc1` mailbox stores per layer (the d8 / de pack it
  replaces is about 14 KB), and C1 still polls one partial per producer per column. The B3 -> C1 hop stays. Worth
  building only as WP17 with a partial-volume design (e.g. per-XCD pre-reduction with the intra-XCD protocol) and the
  ceiling as its bar; tolerance class (T0-T2).
- **Router into M2's tail (N3a).** M2 -> MoE is worth 0.011 hu per D (delta) / up to 0.009 (qsa); N3a moves the
  router GEMV, not the hop; it lives in the rocKE fused MoE (WP2d's lane) and is tolerance class (top-10 flips);
  P15 found the router window latency-bound and bitwise N3b losing. Not cheap; WP16's decision is unchanged.
- **Critical-path islands (lever 4).** The only protocol gain is intra-XCD (4 above); an island of CTAs on one XCD
  still needs `sc1` for every edge to or from the rest of the GPU, and BUBBLES 5 found `sc1` hop latency placement-
  independent in situ. The microbench sees a placement effect at idle (`sc1` round trip 1 : 1.19 : 1.24-1.26 for same
  XCD / same IOD / other IOD), but every all-to-all edge of the layer leaves the island.
- **Hierarchical reductions (lever 5).** On the K=0 chain the only cross-CTA reductions are the spread A1's partials
  (summed by A2 in its own poll, no extra hop) and the fused MoE's combine (rocKE). Nothing to restructure FlyDSL-side.
- **Pre-decode while waiting (lever 7).** There is no decode step: the scaled MFMAs consume MXFP4 / MXFP8 directly,
  and B1, C1, Q1 and M1 issue all their weight loads before their polls already (FLYDSL-STAGES 1). The post-poll tails
  are the MFMA chains the oracles bound (about 0.026 / 0.03 hu for C1 / B1).
- **B1 + B3 head-group fusion (D0 7.2 B1(a); omitted from this section before the fix round).** Not built. The fusion
  would let each B3 task compute the qkvz rows of its own head group, removing the B1 -> B3 hop: B3 is the rocKE
  function (extern-linked), so the cut is a rocKE B1 / B3 restructure (WP2b's lane), and the qkvz projection's weight
  stream, now spread over every B1 CTA, would land on B3's 96 tasks (q / k rows read by the 3 value-head tasks of
  each key head). Its value is bounded by the hop map: layer time is a max over paths of sums of edge times, so it is
  convex in each edge's delay, and removing h of an edge cannot gain more than h times the delay arm's slope: B1 -> B3
  is 0.0088 hu per D (fix-round re-run 0.0087), so the whole hop is worth **at most about 0.011 hu**, before the cost
  of moving B1's chain (its oracle: up to 0.03 hu of B1 work) onto fewer CTAs. Booked as bounded, not built; the
