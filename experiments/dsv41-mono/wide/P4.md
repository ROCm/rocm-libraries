# P4: the wide-concurrency path (TP2, CONC 4-64) of DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash decode

W12 probe P4, 2026-11-03. MI355X (gfx950), TP2 no-EP, DSpark 5, bf16 KV, fp8 index plane, `--level 0` + FULL graphs
(the B0 recipe flags), ATOM main df86c482 in the atom-dev nightly image. Ratios only; absolute numbers and job ids
are in `hackathon-prep/private-perf/dsv41-mono/P4.md` (raw under `raw/p4/`). All runs on `smci355-ccs-aus-n01-09`.

## Verdict

**3x per-request tok/s over ATOM's best served TP2 path is not reachable at CONC 4-16; at CONC 32-64 it is only at the
top edge of the estimate, with the eager-prefill stall removed and every decode-side lever landed.** At wide concurrency the
decode step is routed-MoE bound, and ATOM's MoE already runs at 1.09-1.31x of a rocKE bytes floor at the V4.1 shapes
(the prototype below), so a perfect MoE kernel saves at most 0.08-0.24 of the MoE time. The rest of the decode step
(dense GEMMs, all-reduce, the small-kernel tail) is latency- or link-bound and worth roughly another 0.2-0.3 of the
decode step. The largest single item at CONC >= 16 is not a kernel at all: the served step is 1.5-1.9x the
decode-only GPU step, because every admitted request is prefilled in its own eager step (P1 §3).

| CONC | served step / bytes floor (measured routing) | 3x step / floor | reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 2.85 | 0.95 | infeasible even with every byte at the floor |
| 8 | 3.15 | 1.05 | needs the whole step, prefill included, at 0.95 of the floor: infeasible in practice |
| 16 | 3.84 | 1.28 | needs 0.78 of the floor end to end (today's decode-only step: 0.40) |
| 32 | 4.36 | 1.45 | needs 0.69 of the floor end to end |
| 64 | 5.62 | 1.87 | needs 0.53 of the floor end to end |

Floor = per-rank bytes of a step (target + draft routed experts at the measured unions, dense / replicated weights,
Engram `wkv`, LM head; KV and index bytes omitted, under 0.01) at the rate of the rocKE floor kernel (§5.2). "3x step /
floor" below 1 is impossible; the end-to-end efficiency it needs includes the prefill steps.

Projected from the measured shares (§8; estimates, not measurements): kernel levers alone give about 1.15-1.45x served
at CONC 8-64 (1.3-1.65x on the decode-only step); with the prefill stall also halved about 1.45-1.8x; with it removed
about 1.7-2.1x (C8), 2.0-2.6x (C16), 2.3-2.9x (C32) and 2.5-3.2x (C64).

## 3. Where the step goes as rows grow (decode-only GPU step, rank 0)

Shares of the decode-only span per DSpark step (target + draft, native). "moe" = routed experts of target and draft
including router top-k, sort and MXFP8 quant (aiter's large-M stage-2 kernel `gemm2_a4w4_port` re-attributed from
"gemm" to "moe").

| CONC (target rows) | moe | dense GEMMs | all-reduce / gather | mHC + norm | RoPE / quant | attention | elementwise | indexer | kernels per step |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 (24) | 0.45 | 0.17 | 0.07 | 0.11 | 0.11 | 0.06 | 0.03 | 0.01 | ~1.5 k |
| 8 (48) | 0.50 | 0.15 | 0.09 | 0.09 | 0.09 | 0.05 | 0.02 | 0.01 | ~1.5 k |
| 16 (96) | 0.54 | 0.13 | 0.11 | 0.08 | 0.07 | 0.05 | 0.02 | 0.01 | ~1.5 k |
| 32 (192) | 0.51 | 0.14 | 0.16 | 0.07 | 0.06 | 0.04 | 0.02 | 0.00 | ~1.4 k |
| 64 (384) | 0.47 | 0.13 | 0.22 | 0.07 | 0.05 | 0.06 | 0.01 | 0.00 | ~1.4 k |

Growth of the decode-only span with CONC, relative to CONC 4: 1.25 (C8), 1.59 (C16), 1.99 (C32), 2.71 (C64) for 2, 4,
8, 16x the rows. Native vs mono where mono engages: mono's decode-only span is 0.82 of native's at C4 and 0.95 at C8
(P1: 0.86 / 0.99 on its host timeline); at C >= 16 only native runs (`MAX_ROWS` 48, `MAX_REQUESTS` 8).

Reading:
- **The routed MoE is the largest share at every width (0.45-0.54).** Its kernels are aiter's (FlyDSL `mfma_moe1` /
  `mfma_moe2` up to 96 rows, `..._fp8q_sort` + `gemm2_a4w4_port` from 192 rows: the dsv41 tuned table).
- **All-reduce grows into the second share (0.16 at C32, 0.22 at C64)**: 2 per layer of rows x 10 KiB (§6).
- **The small-kernel tail** (mHC + norm, RoPE / quant, elementwise; about 170 `quantize_fp8` launches a step) is
  0.25 of the span at C4 and still 0.13 at C64: launch- and latency-bound, it is what a wide-row persistent layer removes.
- **Attention and indexer stay small** at these (short, GSM8K-scale) contexts: attention 0.04-0.06, indexer
  0.00-0.01. The sparse attention per layer grows 3.9x from 24 to 384 rows (16x). Long contexts move the indexer
  (it scans L), not covered here.

## 4. Served step vs decode-only step: the admission stall

| CONC | served step / decode-only step (same arm) |
|---|---|
| 4 | 1.05 (native), 1.15 (mono) |
| 8 | 1.23 (native), 1.25 (mono) |
| 16 | 1.53 |
| 32 | 1.72 |
| 64 | 1.90 |

The decode-only window has no admissions, and its requests have longer contexts than GSM8K's (so its step is if
anything slower). The gap grows with the arrival rate per step (CONC / reply length): P1 §3 shows each new request
is prefilled in its own eager, host-launch-bound step, about 14x a CONC-1 mono decode step, stalling every other
request. At CONC >= 32 that stall is about half of the served step: the biggest lever there, and a runtime one
(mixed prefill + decode steps, graph-captured short prefill, or prefill overlapped on a side stream), not a kernel.

## 5. The MoE at 6 x CONC rows

### 5.1 Expert union and tile occupancy (real routing)

| target rows | measured distinct experts / uniform bound | routed (token, slot) pairs per touched expert |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | 0.65 | 1.6 |
| 12 | 0.59 | 1.8 |
| 24 | 0.53 | 2.3 |
| 48 | 0.47 | 3.0 |
| 96 | 0.45 | 4.3 |
| 192 | 0.46 | 6.8 |
| 384 | 0.52 | 11.5 |

GSM8K routing is strongly correlated: about half the uniform union from 24 rows up (draft, 128 experts top-3:
0.28-0.49). A more diverse workload would sit between these and uniform. With 3-12 pairs per expert, a 16- or 32-row
MXFP4 tile is mostly padding: the MoE is a byte stream with a per-expert fixed cost, and that per-expert cost is what
separates ATOM from the floor at small unions (5.2).

### 5.2 Prototype: ATOM's MoE vs the rocKE bytes floor (same job, same GPU)

`dsv41_moe_floor` (256 CTAs x 512 threads, one CTA per CU, 16-byte nontemporal buffer loads, the expert ids staged in
LDS so no dependent global load sits in the stream) streams exactly the touched experts' four regions (`w13`, its
scales, `w2`, its scales) at ATOM's served per-rank layout. Gate: `dbg` builds store every lane's XOR fold and the host
recomputes it from the same bytes, exactly, for all 9 timed arms at two union sizes (18 / 18 equal); the must-fail
twins `skip3` (no `w2`-scale region) and `stride` (wrong expert stride) both differ (4 / 4). Its fastest arm streams
within 1 % of the same rate at every union of 150 experts and up.

ATOM time / floor time, per routed-MoE call (TP2, A-vs-A twins of every floor arm 0.989-1.006, ATOM's own twins
0.986-1.003):

| target rows (CONC) | uniform | hot (Zipf) | real (captured) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 (1) | 1.62 | 1.63 | 1.53 |
| 12 (2) | 1.12 | 1.22 | 1.22 |
| 24 (4) | 1.18 | 1.23 | 1.34 |
| 48 (8) | 1.16 | 1.20 | 1.27 |
| 96 (16) | 1.13 | 1.17 | 1.31 |
| 192 (32) | 1.09 | 1.13 | 1.21 |
| 384 (64) | 1.11 | 1.16 | 1.31 |

So the best any MoE kernel can do at these shapes is 0.76-0.83 of ATOM's MoE time with real routing (0.86-0.92 with
uniform routing). In the served step the MoE family also carries the top-k, sort and quant launches (about 0.17 of the
family at C16) that a fused kernel would fold. P1 measures the same 1.2x for the routed bucket at C4-C16 on both the
native and the mono path.

Combined (estimates; the -0.25 to -0.40 applied at every CONC): C8 served 1.25-1.45x (kernels only), 1.45-1.7x (stall
halved), 1.7-2.1x (stall removed); C16 1.2-1.35 / 1.5-1.75 / 2.0-2.6x; C32 1.15-1.3 / 1.55-1.8 / 2.3-2.9x; C64

## 9. Side findings

- **Pad of the routed experts.** `Mxfp4MoEMethod` defaults to `pad_align` 256, but V4 / V4.1 construct `FusedMoE` with
  `pad_align=128` (`atom/models/deepseek_v4.py`), so the served TP2 intermediate is 1152 unpadded, which is the shape
  aiter's dsv41 tuned table is keyed on. A 256 pad (1280) runs 1.03-1.18x slower at 6-384 rows (same weights, same
  process; A-vs-A 0.976-1.007), with other kernels from 192 rows. Any V4.1-shaped MoE bench or knob must keep 128.
  TP4's 576 pads to 640 (+0.11 bytes) under the same rule.
- **Numerics of the MoE bench** (ATOM's kernel, not a gate of ours): output vs a float64 reference with emulated MXFP8
  activations, relative L2 1.6-1.75e-2 (pad 256 and the served pad up to 96 rows) and 3.6-6.5e-3 (served pad from 192
  rows: another tuned kernel); run-to-run relative L2 0.6-3.4e-3 (stage-2 atomics); a one-scale mutant moves the max
  error 5-30x above that floor.
- **Graph capture does not keep inputs alive**: an earlier bench round lost its uniform-routing arms to freed routing
  tensors (alola-cluster rule 249); the data above is from the fixed bench.
- **TP1 per-rank MoE shape** (intermediate 2304, for P3): the same bench gives the Triton-streamer floor / ATOM time 0.90-1.0
  from 24 rows up and 0.95-1.0 from 48 (uniform / skewed routing), i.e. about 0.88-0.93 of the rocKE floor's speed.

## 10. Caveats

- Routing is GSM8K's (one domain, short replies); diverse traffic touches more experts and raises every floor.
- One node, one GPU per arm; the MoE-vs-floor ratios are same-job, same-GPU; served bands per §2.
- The decode-only windows run with the kineto profiler on (1.01-1.05x on P1's cross-check).
- The §8 levers are estimates from measured shares and the Qwen kernels' measured floor fractions; none is built or
  served yet.
