# P1: where the DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash decode step goes (TP2, DSpark, mono vs native)

W12 probe P1, 2026-11-03. MI355X (gfx950), TP2 no-EP, DSpark 5 draft tokens, bf16 KV, fp8 index plane, `--level 0`
+ FULL graphs (the B0 recipe flags), GSM8K prompts as served in B0. Ratios only; absolute numbers are in
`hackathon-prep/private-perf/dsv41-mono/P1-abs.md`.

## Verdict

The decode step is GPU-bound at every concurrency: the inter-step GPU gap is about 0.1% of a step, and the
target graph is 0.87-0.93 of it. Against an HBM bytes floor built from **measured** routing, the best ATOM step is
**2.1-2.4x the floor at every concurrency**. On top of that, **eager single-request prefills stall every other
request's decode for 0.12-0.28 of its window at CONC 2-16**.

Together these cap a decode-kernel-only rewrite at about **2.4x (C1), 2.4x (C2), 2.7x (C4), 3.0x (C8) and 3.05x (C16)**
over the best served path. That cap assumes 100% of the achievable HBM stream and zero prefill stall. At a realistic
85% of the stream, a 3x gain also needs about 1.2-1.5x fewer bytes per accepted token: lower-precision attention,
shared-expert and LM-head weights, less verify width, or a higher acceptance length.

ATOM's mono gain collapses at CONC >= 8 for two reasons:
- **CONC 16 never runs mono.** 96 verify rows exceed `MAX_ROWS` = 48, and 16 draft requests exceed `MAX_REQUESTS` = 8.
- **At CONC 8 (48 rows) mono runs but ties native (step ratio 0.99).** The routed MoE is then 0.45 of the step, and
  both paths run it at the same rate (1.2x its floor). Mono's attention, attention-output and K1 stages scale worse
  with rows than native's kernels. That cancels what mono saves on launch latency.

Driver: ATOM's offline engine (`p1_driver.py`), with the B0 client's prompt set and warm-up at closed concurrency with
refill. A fresh engine is started per arm-run. Its per-request decode rates match served B0 within 0-7% at every cell
(private table), except a mono C16 run with one compile stall, which is excluded.

Timing discipline:
- **A-vs-A twin:** native m0 vs m0b decode-step medians agree within 0.5% at every bs.
- **Profiler cross-check:** the kernel-trace step spans are 1.01-1.05x the no-profiler GPU-event steps (marker and
  profiler cost). Per segment at C1 mono, kernel trace / no profiler: target graph 1.02, draft 1.06.

## 2. The step: segments (host timeline, no profiler)

Shares of the decode step at bs = CONC (median step, GPU time).

| Segment | mono C1 | mono C4 | mono C8 | native C1 | native C4 | native C8 | native C16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| target graph (+ logits) | 0.874 | 0.914 | 0.927 | 0.891 | 0.913 | 0.925 | 0.929 |
| draft graph (3 stages + LM head + Markov) | 0.089 | 0.064 | 0.058 | 0.085 | 0.068 | 0.060 | 0.055 |
| eager tail between graphs (prepare, draft-KV write, sampler, staging) | 0.035 | 0.020 | 0.015 | 0.022 | 0.017 | 0.014 | 0.013 |
| inter-step GPU gap | 0.0011 | 0.0006 | 0.0004 | 0.0007 | 0.0005 | 0.0004 | 0.0003 |

- **Host idle is about 0.** The GPU never drains between steps (`q` = 0-0.17 and the gap is about 6 us).
- **Host work is hidden, not absent.** The runner blocks at the start of a step until the previous step's GPU work
  ends. Its own work after that runs under the target graph:
  - graph launch (native's 1.3k-node graph costs 4x mono's)
  - Python draft-KV write
  - sampler bookkeeping
  - draft staging

  That work is **0.25 of the mono C1 step (0.39 for native)**. At a 3x faster step it would be 0.74 of the step, so
  the design must remove it from the critical path: capture the eager tail, launch asynchronously.

## 3. The cell: decode vs prefill

Each new request is prefilled in its own eager step (pf = 1; it is not mixed into a decode step). Such a step costs
**14x a C1 mono decode step**. It is host-launch bound:
- The lagging rank's GPU is busy only about 0.21 of the prefill span.
- The other rank spends about 0.70 of the span inside all-reduce kernels, waiting.

| Cell | share of GPU time in prefill steps (mono / native / native twin) | foreign-prefill stall in a request's decode window, approx. pf x (c-1)/c |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | 0.19 / 0.13 / 0.08 | 0 (own prefill is outside the decode rate) |
| C2 | 0.24 / 0.17 / 0.26 | 0.12 |
| C4 | 0.26 / 0.21 / 0.21 | 0.20 |
| C8 | 0.30 / 0.29 / 0.26 | 0.25 |
| C16 | (artefact) / 0.29 / 0.32 | 0.28 |

GSM8K replies are short (median ~0.17k tokens), so each request pays one ~0.1k-token prefill. That is why this bucket
is so large. It is the single largest non-decode bucket from C4 up. It is identical for mono and native: prefill
never runs mono.

and CCL C1 values as about ±0.1 of the step.

How to read these multiples:
- **Already at the floor:** the routed MoE from C4 up (1.2x on both paths), the LM heads (1.0x) and Engram (about 1x).
  These are the byte-bound buckets.
- **Latency- or structure-bound (tens to hundreds of times their bytes):**
  - mHC seams, router, indexer, attention core
  - CCL, the shared expert and the attention projections at C >= 4 (4-8x)
  - the draft backbone (4-7x)

  Together these are **0.45-0.60 of the step at every CONC**. That is the kernel-side headroom: fusion,
  persistence, and an in-kernel CCL with tagged mailboxes.

## 6. Why the mono's gain collapses at CONC >= 8

1. **Hard limits.**
   - `MAX_ROWS` = 48 (`mono/config.py`): above it the target step runs native. A TP2 shared expert past 48 rows
     needs more than one unit per CTA, and K1 / K2 spill.
   - `MAX_REQUESTS` = 8: above it the draft runs native.
   - DSpark verify is 6 rows per request, so CONC >= 9 is fully native. C16 (96 rows, 16 draft requests) never runs
     mono: mono / native step = 1.00.
2. **Graph-ladder padding.** The capture sizes [1..8, 16, 32, 48, 64] pad a batch of 9-15 requests to 16. Every
   step with 9-15 requests costs a 16-request step (measured: bs 9-15 steps are 0.92-0.99 of a bs-16 step). Mono's
   48-row limit makes the jump from bs 8 to 9 cost 1.23-1.27x in step time while active rows grow 1.125x.
3. **At 48 rows mono is a tie (0.99), not a win.**
   - The step's bytes are about 0.84 routed MoE.
   - Both paths stream the routed weights at the same rate (1.2x floor): mono's routed share 0.45 vs native's 0.42
     of almost equal steps.
   - Mono's fixed-launch saving is roughly constant in absolute terms (about 0.39 of a C1 native step: 33 small
     kernels per layer at a few us each, 80 all-reduces, a 4x costlier graph launch). At 48 rows that saving is only
     about 0.24 of the step.
   - It is eaten by stages that scale worse in mono than in native:
     - **attention core:** 1.8x native's at C8. The bit-matched split-K attention keeps the 6-row reproducibility
       order, 64 splits x 16 keys.
     - **attention post:** 1.34x native's.
     - **K1:** per layer 2x (reuse) to 3x (index layers) its 6-row time.
     - **K2's non-routed part:** doubles per layer from 6 to 48 rows (eager-stamp split).
4. **Fallbacks around the mono kernels.** In every mono target step the compressor, Engram, candidate table,
   embedding, step metadata and all-gather still run as native launches (0.08 / 0.05 / 0.04 of the step at
   C1 / C4 / C8). The whole eager tail and the draft LM head / Markov sampler (5 sequential all-gathers) are outside
   the mono too.
5. **Prefill** (§3) is never mono and is 0.26-0.30 of the cell at C8 / C16 on both paths. Even an infinitely fast
   decode cannot exceed 1 / (1 - stall share) there.

How the work scales per bucket, from C1 to C16:
- **Routed experts:** grow with the distinct experts (22 → 134 per layer). This is the only bucket that tracks bytes.
- **Attention core and attention post:** grow with rows.
- **mHC, router, indexer, CCL, Engram, compressor, step meta:** roughly flat per step (latency-bound).
- **Draft:** grows with requests but stays about 0.05 of the step.

## 7. Budget table for the design

Ratios are relative to the **best measured decode step at that CONC** (mono for C1-C4, native for C8 / C16).
"Stall" = the foreign-prefill share of a request's decode window. A 3x per-request rate at the same acceptance
length needs (step + stall) to fall to 1/3 of today's.

| | C1 | C2 | C4 | C8 | C16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| floor / best step (measured routing) | 0.42 | 0.47 | 0.47 | 0.45 | 0.45 |
| stall share today | 0 | 0.12 | 0.20 | 0.25 | 0.28 |
| ceiling: step at 100% floor, stall 0 | 2.39x | 2.44x | 2.66x | 3.0x | 3.05x |
| ceiling at 85% of the stream | 2.0x | 2.1x | 2.3x | 2.55x | 2.6x |
| bytes per accepted token cut needed for 3x at 85% | 1.48x | 1.45x | 1.33x | 1.18x | 1.16x |
| host own work / best step today (must fit in the 3x step) | 0.25 | 0.19 | 0.13 | 0.09 (mono) / 0.14 (native) | 0.11 |

The per-bucket budget for a 3x step follows from §4-5. It is the step's floor (0.42-0.47 of today's step), plus a
latency allowance, minus the byte cuts:
- **Byte-bound buckets (already near floor; only byte cuts help):**
  - routed MoE: 0.58-0.88 of the floor
  - attention projections: 0.19-0.05
  - LM heads: 0.10-0.03
  - shared expert: 0.05-0.01
  - Engram wkv: 0.02
- **Latency-bound buckets (0.45-0.60 of today's step): must shrink to about their floors (well under 0.05 of the
  3x step).**
  - mHC, router, indexer, CCL, attention core, draft backbone, eager tail
  - That means one persistent kernel per step (or per layer group): in-kernel CCL with tagged mailboxes and holds,
    fused seams, a fused router / top-k, the eager tail captured, and the draft folded into the same launch.
- **Prefill:** take it off the decode critical path, or make it cheap:
  - batch refills
  - mix chunked prefill rows into the decode step (rows <= the step's width)
  - graph-capture the small-prefill path (today its span is about 4.5x the lagging rank's GPU work)

  The stall share at C4-C16 (0.20-0.28) is the second-largest lever there.
- **Candidate byte cuts for D0, each needs the GSM8K + long-context accuracy gate:**
  - attention projections FP8 → MXFP4 (about -0.09 of the C1 floor)
  - target / draft LM head bf16 → FP8 or MXFP4 (-0.05..-0.08)
  - draft head on a top-k vocabulary or reuse of the target logits (-0.05)
  - shared expert FP8 → MXFP4 (-0.025)
  - shard the replicated tensors across the 2 ranks: wqkv_a, router, mHC, Engram wkv, main_proj (-0.04)
  - fewer verify rows per request when confidence is low (DSpark confidence head; cuts the distinct experts)
  - a higher acceptance length

## 8. Caveats and what is not done

- **The mono K1 / K2 split comes from eager stamps.** In eager mode the ranks are skewed, so the peer-sum stage
  (`slice`) absorbs waiting. The rank-1 (less waiting) split is used. Graph-mode K1 / K2 totals per layer are exact
  from the kernel trace.
  - A graph-mode stage split needs a saver for the timeline buffer after a graph replay. It is not built.
  - It matters only for dividing mono's K2 between routed / CCL / attention; native buckets are exact.
- **Routing comes from P4's eager native run on rank 0, at C8-C64 cells.** Rows 6-48 occur in the drain of those
  cells. The prompt mix is the same GSM8K set.
- **Kernel-trace windows are 2 s per cell** (5-300 steps per bs). The C2 decomposition comes from the host timeline
  only.
- **Each phase ran on one node** (host timeline / mono timeline on n01-09, kernel trace on n07-17). P1 claims no
  end-to-end win, so the both-nodes rule does not apply. The two nodes agree on step times within 0-5% (the trace
  adds marker cost).
- **Short-context workload** (GSM8K, ~0.1k + 0.17k tokens): the indexer and attention bytes are tiny. Long-context
  cells belong to P4 (wide CONC).
